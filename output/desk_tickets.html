Reflection

Every point below is from the ticket tabs and the Cash tab on this page, plus the desk that ran those tickets.

How the agents performed

101 went smooth and seamless, without issue. On the 101 tab the Boss called Inventory first. Inventory counted CC-TEE-WHITE size S at 0, and Accounting read invoice 501, the open $840 reprint. The Cash tab shows ticket 101 at $0.00. The bill was prepared and not paid, and checking stayed $3,400.00. Facilities stayed off that ticket.

102 went smooth and seamless, without issue. On the 102 tab the Boss called Facilities first. Facilities read lease 1, and Accounting prepared the $2,400 rent without mailing a check. The Cash tab shows ticket 102 at $0.00. Inventory and Customer Service stayed off that ticket.

103 presented a limit issue, so we had to rework how we address those matters so the Boss is not called too many times. On the 103 tab the Boss stopped on the four-call cap. Accounting was asked and refused, and the Cash tab shows ticket 103 at $0.00 because that ticket had no invoice and no lease. The rework is one call from the Boss to Inventory, then Customer Service, then Accounting, and the invoice comes back up the chain instead of calling the Boss again.

Actual against the plan

101 worked as expected. The Expected side says Inventory first, then the customer draft, then Accounting on invoice 501. The Actual side is Boss to Inventory to Accounting, with the stock tools and read_vendor_invoice. Customer Service was refused once the handoff cap was hit. The Cash tab still shows $0.00, so the $840 reprint was not the sale.

102 worked as expected. The Expected side says Facilities first, then Accounting schedules the $2,400 and does not mail it. The Actual side is that handoff, using read_lease, read_shop_date, and read_cash_accounts. The Cash tab shows $0.00 and no check mailed.

103 needed a more efficient process, and that process should be saved as a pathway for similar issues. Expected said Customer Service first, with Inventory and Accounting, and read_sku_price for the discount. Actual started at Inventory, Accounting never got in, and the price tool was not used. The Cash tab had nothing to move. The saved pathway is the one Boss call, then the chain, so a short rack and a bulk price do not burn the four-call cap.

What one agent could have done

103 could have been handled solely by the Boss or the human, because it required human thinking to resolve the matter. The 103 tab shows the team counting size M at 8 against an ask of 20 and then dying on the cap before a price. The Cash tab confirms the tools had no bill to pay. The human had to decide the order stays 20 size M, at 10% off because it is 20 or more, and that the 12 short are a reprint. One person with check_size_on_hand and read_sku_price could have put that choice on the desk. The team path is what made the Boss get called until the limit.

Three problems this team could take

Campus Customs can run out of inventory, and now it can replenish and keep certain pars in production versus sales. The 101 tab already shows that hole: size S qty 0. The 103 tab shows the other hole: size M qty 8 against 20. check_size_on_hand and list_sizes_on_hand are the tools that see the par break. The 103 pathway is how the shortfall gets priced and handed to the human instead of bouncing the Boss.

Customer Service needs the functionality to queue emails and to punch down solutions, like recommending alternative styles. On the 101 tab the customer note is a draft on the board, and no tool sends it. On the 103 tab the same rule holds: the club reply stays a draft. The size list is already a tool, so an alternative style can be named from stock the way the 103 rack listed the other sizes. The queue itself is the missing send button, which is why the Cash tab never shows a sale just because a draft was written.

Accounting needs a calendar that is shared globally for when recurring bills come up, so we do not stop production because the rent is not paid or the vendors are not paid. The 102 tab is the rent: lease 1, $2,400, due two days after the shop date. The 101 tab is the vendor bill: invoice 501, $840, due 2026-08-28, before the shop date 2026-08-31. The Cash tab shows both at $0.00 after the runs, prepared and not paid, while checking stayed $3,400.00. read_lease, read_vendor_invoice, read_shop_date, and read_cash_accounts are how Accounting already sees those dates. The shared calendar is so those two bills surface before the rack goes quiet.

Three problems this team could not take

Campus Customs needs an app with a shopper experience. None of the three ticket tabs is a shopper screen. 101, 102, and 103 are desk tickets, and the Cash tab only moves when a human approves a bill the tools already found. There is no cart, no customer login, and no agent whose job is the shopper.

Campus Customs needs client feedback and reviews on the website. The 101 and 103 tabs let Customer Service write a draft and leave it on the board. Nothing on those tabs, or on the Cash tab, stores a review or a rating. That would take a review tool Customer Service does not have.

Campus Customs needs to showcase seasonal releases through media like reels that are top-notch productions. The tools on the ticket tabs read stock, price, leases, invoices, and cash. The Cash tab is a till, not a studio. A reel needs a media tool and a person who produces it. This desk does not have either one.
